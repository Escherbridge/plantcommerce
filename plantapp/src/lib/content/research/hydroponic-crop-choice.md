**Choosing crops for a hydroponic system**

# Choosing crops for a hydroponic system

A tomato is not a lettuce with patience. Official pages already sorted the pairing: leafy, short-rooted crops in films, rafts, and non-circulating Kratky; long-season fruiting crops in drip-fed media. EC and pH then come from a labeled table, not from a blended house average and not from a wish to grow everything in one channel. This page is a crop-to-system guide, not a seed catalog.

## Hero figures, labeled by source

Oklahoma State University HLA-6722, Table 2, “Optimum range of electrical conductivity (EC) and pH values for hydroponic crops,” lists crop-specific bands in mS/cm (equivalent to dS/m). The edible subset below is OSU’s table, not a universal law, and it is not flattened against other official ranges. The same fact sheet’s general solution discussion often sits around pH 5–6 (commonly 5.5). That general band and the tomato row (pH 6.0–6.5) do not need to be forced into one invented “ideal.” Units are mS/cm. One millisiemens per centimeter equals 1,000 microsiemens per centimeter.

| Crop | EC (mS/cm) | pH |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basil | 1.0–1.6 | 5.5–6.0 |
| Cucumber | 1.7–2.0 | 5.0–5.5 |
| Lettuce | 1.2–1.8 | 6.0–7.0 |
| Peppers | 0.8–1.8 | 5.5–6.0 |
| Tomato | 2.0–4.0 | 6.0–6.5 |

University of Kentucky CCD-CP-63 (2025) gives an example lettuce fertigation recipe “with electrical conductivity around 1.5 mS/cm.” That sits inside OSU’s lettuce EC band (1.2–1.8). UKY also recommends bonded 1-inch plugs for DWC and NFT lettuce. Those are Kentucky lettuce figures, not a tomato recipe.

Other rows exist in OSU’s table, including ornamentals; they are not dumped here because they drown the vegetable decision. How to take the reading, and why official tables do not all agree, belongs on the [EC and pH](/guides/hydroponic-ec-ph-nutrients) page. A [source-water test](/guides/source-water-testing) should happen before anyone chases a table number.

## System pairing

Oregon State University Extension EM 9457 (May 2025) again: NFT channels “are best suited for short-lived or small-rooted crops such as herbs and leafy greens.” UF/IFAS HS785 / CV263 (revised September 2018): “Lettuce is particularly adapted to NFT because it is a short-term crop and less subject to damage from root rot.” The same publication describes tomato in 4-inch PVC NFT as confining, prone to oxygen problems and Pythium, and says “essentially all PVC tomato growers have since switched to bag or other soilless media culture.” That is the official crop-to-system pairing. The [nutrient film technique](/guides/nutrient-film-technique) guide already told the pipe story; this page is where the pairing becomes the point.

University of Hawaiʻi–CTAHR Kratky VC-1 (September 2010) limits the non-circulating method to “crops that require less than 2 gallons of water per plant for the entire growing season, from transplanting to harvest.” Potential crops listed there include “leafy, romaine, and semi-head lettuces, cilantro,” and other short-season examples, not long-season tomatoes. The method itself is on the [Kratky](/guides/kratky-method) page.

Tomatoes, peppers, and cucumbers belong, in the official Florida production picture, in a Dutch bucket, a lay-flat bag, or a slab. That layout, and the leachate rule that goes with it, is on the [drip and Dutch-bucket](/guides/drip-dutch-bucket) page. [Choosing a first hydroponic system](/guides/choosing-hydroponic-system) is the comparison menu.

UF/IFAS HS405 (original January 2010) reports a Florida greenhouse-crop ranking from the industry snapshot in that publication, “ordered from most grown to least grown”: herbs, peppers, cucumbers, tomatoes, lettuce, mixed vegetable/herb, and strawberries. That is a dated Florida survey ranking, not current national market share, and not a recommendation list for an Intermountain greenhouse.

Official pages do not forbid mixed plantings. They repeatedly pair short-rooted leafy crops with films and rafts, and fruiting crops with media and drip. If you mix, say so as a grower choice, not as an official recipe.

## Common mistakes

Forcing tomatoes into a system sized for lettuce is the first (UF’s PVC history; Kratky’s gallon cap). Averaging OSU’s crop rows into one invented “ideal EC” is the second. Treating the 2010-era Florida ranking as a national or Intermountain recommendation is the third. Ignoring that lettuce and tomato pH rows in the same official table do not match is the fourth. Skipping a source-water test before chasing a table number is the fifth.

## What to do with the usual questions

**What are the best plants for hydroponics, or for NFT?** Official leaning: lettuce and herbs in NFT, deep water culture, and non-circulating Kratky; tomatoes in drip-fed media. Kratky’s own cap is crops that require less than 2 gallons of water per plant from transplanting to harvest.

**What EC for lettuce versus tomatoes?** OSU Table 2: lettuce 1.2–1.8 mS/cm, tomato 2.0–4.0 mS/cm. UKY’s example lettuce recipe is about 1.5. Do not collapse those bands.

**Can one system grow both?** Official pages do not forbid it, but they repeatedly sort the crops as above. A mid-season root mat in a narrow shared channel is the ending UF already wrote for tomatoes in lettuce pipe.
