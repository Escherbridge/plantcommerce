**Starting seeds and transplants for soilless systems**

# Starting seeds and transplants for soilless systems

A transplant is how pests commute. Official lettuce handbooks already specify how to start a soilless plug, and official tunnel and hydroponic production guides already say not to walk a diseased or infested tray into the system. A garden-center six-pack is not interchangeable with a clean soilless transplant. Official lettuce programs already disagree slightly on nursery EC. They agree on one seed per plug, a pre-wetted substrate, humidity that comes off after emergence, and a hard no on diseased trays. This page is a plug-starting guide, not a seed catalog, and not a blended house recipe.

## Two official lettuce programs, side by side

Every number below is a named lettuce-nursery program, not a universal home schedule for every crop.

**Cornell University CEA, *Hydroponic Lettuce Handbook* (2013).** Rockwool is “moistened with nutrient solution that has a relatively low pH such as 4.5 to remove pockets of high pH contaminants.” Trays may sit on an ebb-and-flood bench, a table, or a float. Day 0: 20 °C (68 °F), 50 µmol/m²/s, a 24-hour photoperiod, humidity covers, and “Trays on an Ebb and Flood bench are sub-irrigated with RO water for 1/4 hour every 12 hours.” Day 1: fertilizer starts, “EC 1200 µS/cm above source,” pH 5.8, 25 °C, 250 µmol/m²/s, still a 24-hour photoperiod. Day 2: humidity covers come off. Days 3–4: remove double seedlings and oversize plants. Day 6: “The sub-irrigation system if using an ebb and flood table is scheduled for flooding four times per day, or every six hours, for 1/4 hr (15 min).” One seed per plug is the handbook’s practice. The bench itself is described in the [ebb-and-flow](/guides/ebb-and-flow) guide. 1,200 µS/cm equals 1.2 mS/cm; that is still Cornell’s “above source” lettuce target, not a reason to average it with the Kentucky program below.

**University of Kentucky CCD-CP-63 (2025)** is a second official lettuce-propagation program. “For water-based systems like deep water culture (DWC) or nutrient film technique (NFT), bonded 1-inch plugs are strongly recommended” because they “will not fall apart when submerged.” “Substrates, particularly rockwool, should be pre-charged with a nutrient solution using a balanced complete fertilizer with an EC of 1.0 mS/cm and a pH of 5.5-6.0 before seeding. Place one seed per plug/cell. Lettuce seeds germinate best at a temperature of 68 °F (20 °C) and high relative humidity over 88%.” A germination dome “should be removed as soon as the seeds have emerged, about two days after seeding.” Light: “around 50 µmol/m²/s to initiate germination,” then “gradually increase to 200–300 µmol/m²/s as seedlings develop.” “Seedlings should be watered at least once per day with a nutrient solution using a balanced complete fertilizer with an EC of 1.0 mS/cm and a pH of 5.5-6.0, depending on plant sizes and growing environments.” The same bulletin already says “do not introduce diseased plugs” into production; that line is also used in the [troubleshooting](/guides/hydroponic-troubleshooting-root-rot) guide.

| | Cornell 2013 lettuce nursery | Kentucky CCD-CP-63 (2025) lettuce |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Substrate wet-up | Rockwool moistened at a relatively low pH, such as 4.5 | Pre-charge, especially rockwool, at 1.0 mS/cm, pH 5.5–6.0 |
| Fertilizer EC | Day 1: 1,200 µS/cm above source water, pH 5.8 | 1.0 mS/cm, pH 5.5–6.0, before seeding and in daily watering |
| Humidity | Covers on Days 0–1; off on Day 2 | Dome optional for high humidity; off about two days after seeding, once seeds have emerged |
| Temperature / light | Day 0: 20 °C, 50 µmol/m²/s, 24-hour photoperiod. Day 1: 25 °C, 250 µmol/m²/s | Germination at 68 °F (20 °C), RH over 88%; about 50 µmol/m²/s, then 200–300 µmol/m²/s |
| Watering | Ebb-and-flood: 15 minutes every 12 hours (RO) through Day 5; from Day 6, 15 minutes every six hours | At least once per day with the 1.0 mS/cm solution |
| Seeds | One seed per plug; remove doubles on Days 3–4 | One seed per plug or cell |

Those two EC targets are not the same instruction. Cornell’s 1,200 µS/cm is above source water. Kentucky’s 1.0 mS/cm is a pre-charge and watering strength. This page prints both and does not average them into a house nursery number.

## Into the system, and what not to walk in the door

University of Hawaiʻi–CTAHR Kratky VC-1 (September 2010) starts seedlings in net pots for 1–3 weeks before they go onto the non-circulating tank. The tank itself is on the [Kratky](/guides/kratky-method) page. UF/IFAS HS785 / CV263 (revised September 2018): NFT transplants are “usually started in rockwool or foam cubes.” In true NFT, “the plastic liner is wrapped around the stems to prevent algae growth in the channel.” The [nutrient film technique](/guides/nutrient-film-technique) guide covers the channel.

Penn State Extension, “High Tunnel Production” (updated 13 March 2023), and University of Kentucky ID-235 (revised October 2018) both say to inspect every incoming transplant. Penn State: inspect all transplants before planting and remove plants with aphids, whiteflies, thrips, lesions, or weeds in the trays. UKY: inspect new plants coming into structures; a 2-week plant-free period before a new crop is part of that guide’s sanitation list. Dirty trays are an IPM problem, not only a germination problem. Details live in the [greenhouse and high-tunnel IPM](/guides/greenhouse-ipm) guide.

The opened official soilless lettuce programs use bonded plugs or rockwool, not a washed field mix. This page does not invent a soil-to-hydro wash protocol that those pages do not give.

## Common mistakes

Averaging Cornell’s 1,200 µS/cm-above-source and Kentucky’s 1.0 mS/cm into one invented nursery EC is the first. Leaving a humidity dome on after emergence is the second. Two seeds left in one plug is the third (both official lettuce programs start at one, and Cornell removes doubles). Walking uninspected garden-center plants into a closed loop or tunnel is the fourth. Letting rockwool dry, or never pre-charging it, is the fifth.

## What to do with the usual questions

**How do I start seeds in rockwool, and when do I transplant lettuce?** Official starting picture: one seed in a small bonded or rockwool plug, pre-wetted, under a short high-humidity period, then into the system once roots and a true leaf stage the grower can handle. Kratky’s publication uses a 1–3 week nursery window. This page does not invent days-to-transplant for crops the opened pages do not schedule.

**Do I need a humidity dome?** Cornell uses covers for Days 0–1 and removes them on Day 2. Kentucky: a dome is optional for high humidity and comes off about two days after seeding, once seeds have emerged.

**Can I start in potting soil and wash the roots?** The opened official soilless lettuce programs use bonded plugs or rockwool. No wash protocol is given there.
